# v0.1.41 - Task and Capability Governance

> Status: Planned
> Features: F089, F090, F091
> Depends on: F116 Runtime Host Adapter, F121 Coder Shared Daemon and Multi-Client Live State, and F130 Partner Outcome-First Workspace

## Batch decision

Space maintains one normalized projection of Runtime-owned task, capability, and route facts, then presents those facts according to the active product surface:

- Coder keeps the Task Dock and Plan popout for plan, progress, capability health, and effective run facts.
- Partner presents plan/progress, current-run degradation, warnings, and the completion receipt in F130 `Process`; generated objects, preview, versions, verification, and export stay in `Results`.
- Full capability, Skill, MCP, provider/model, and resolved-route facts live in expandable technical details or Settings/Diagnostics. Partner does not gain a permanent capability panel or another top-level rail.

`SurfaceRuntimeRouter` selects the authoritative owner before facts are normalized. Coder task, Todo, catalog, status, route, and capability facts come from the F121 daemon projection. Partner facts come from the F116 embedded-inline projection plus named Space bridges. No Coder component subscribes to or falls back to the embedded Runtime owner.

Space displays Runtime-owned facts and explicit host policy. It does not create a parallel task engine, capability registry, provider router, or user-visible `Plan` work mode. F130 outcome entries remain optional result shortcuts over the existing automatic execution path.

The Partner session/run receipt taxonomy used by F130/F089/F091 contains three distinct user-facing receipt types. Their persistence follows each authoritative owner; this taxonomy does not imply three Space-owned stores:

- **Outcome receipt (F130)** is exactly one immutable Space-owned sidecar per Partner session. It records the bounded task initiator plus an optional result entry and desired deliverable when an interactive Partner turn is first accepted or F097 commits pre-dispatch preparation, before requesting Runtime dispatch; it is presentation metadata, not execution truth.
- **Completion receipt (F089)** is one idempotent typed evidence snapshot/projection per admitted terminal `(sessionId, runId)`. It summarizes that run's terminal plan state and links claims to available evidence; latest-per-session is derived while older run receipts remain retained under owner policy. It never becomes or rewrites Runtime Todo truth.
- **Route receipt (F091)** is one ordered Runtime-owned, run-correlated projection per admitted run when public facts exist. It reports effective provider/model/effort/fallback/assurance facts, becomes terminal-frozen for that run, and remains absent or explicitly partial when no public payload exists. It explains execution after routing; it never becomes a route selector or a Space-authored routing store.

An Outcome receipt is written exactly once for a Partner session after bounded pre-dispatch outcome inference. If no truthful entry can be resolved before dispatch, its origin remains `unspecified` for the lifetime of that receipt; later results never rewrite it. `AutomationDefinitionRevision` and `AutomationRunRecord` are durable scheduling and audit records, not additional session/run receipt types. An automation execution may link Outcome, Completion, and Route receipts without merging their ownership or payloads.

Partner UI naming is fixed:

- `Task plan` is the F089 Runtime-owned plan section inside Process.
- `Plan` remains the Coder popout label and is never a Partner top-level tab or work mode.
- `Completion summary` is the concise center message and carries no separate durable authority.
- `Completion receipt` is F089's terminal evidence-linked object in Process; an exported receipt becomes a Result.

## FEATURE_089: Task Plan Timeline and Completion Receipt

### User outcome

Users can see the current plan, bounded changes to that plan, drift warnings, completed/blocked/skipped work, and a run completion receipt that links claims to tool, test, diff, artifact, Workflow, and External Agent evidence.

### Contract and design

- Consume normalized `todo.updated` and `todo.warning` facts through `SurfaceRuntimeRouter`: F121 daemon projection for Coder and F116 embedded-inline projection for Partner. Normalize only after owner identity, sequence, and session admission have been validated.
- Maintain one normalized renderer projection consumed by the Coder Plan popout/Task Dock and Partner `Process`; surface components may change density and actions but cannot disagree about current facts.
- Record a bounded in-session change timeline with event sequence/time and source correlation.
- Restore only data supported by transcript/Runtime state. Do not persist a Space-authored Todo truth that can diverge from KodaX.
- Completion receipt sections:
  - requested outcome;
  - final plan status;
  - changed files/diffs when available;
  - verification/tool evidence;
  - created artifacts/deliveries;
  - Workflow/External Agent tasks;
  - blockers, limitations, and unverified claims.
- Copy/export creates an artifact from the receipt snapshot; it does not mutate the plan.
- In Partner, `Process` owns the full completion receipt and its evidence navigation. The center may show only F130's concise completion summary with a link to `Process`; any exported receipt becomes a result in `Results`.
- An F130 outcome entry or Outcome receipt cannot create, select, edit, or imply a Runtime plan.
- F129's presentation Story is result-scoped project state owned by Presentation Studio. It must not be written into, inferred from, or presented as the F089 Runtime Task plan.

### Gate

Current-state and in-session timeline can ship on Runtime events. Durable todo editing/querying across hosts remains out of scope until KodaX exposes a public todo service; KX-F113 is a watchlist dependency, not a hidden assumption.

### Acceptance

- Coder Plan/Task Dock and Partner `Process` use the same normalized fact model but accept facts only from their surface-selected owner; two renderer components cannot disagree within one admitted session.
- Empty todo update clears current state without deleting terminal per-run Completion receipts retained under policy.
- Drift warnings are session-scoped and recoverable.
- Completion receipt references available evidence and labels missing verification.
- Resume behavior is explicitly current-only or durable according to the SDK contract actually present.
- Partner can complete a task without ever choosing or seeing a `Plan` work mode.
- Partner exposes Task plan only as a Process section; no `Plan` tab or mode is introduced.
- The center Completion summary, F089 Completion receipt, F130 Outcome receipt, and F129 Story remain separately typed and cannot overwrite or masquerade as one another.
- A two-runs-one-session restart/idempotency fixture restores one Outcome receipt, two separately keyed Completion receipts, and two ordered Route projections without overwriting, duplicating, or treating latest-per-session as storage truth.

## FEATURE_090: Runtime Capability Health and Resume Diagnostics

### User outcome

When a session is resumed or a run degrades, users can tell which MCP servers, Extensions, deferred tools, Skills, External Agents, and Runtime services are available, missing, stale, reconnecting, or policy-disabled.

### Contract and design

Consume:

- `runtime.catalog` providers/models/commands/skills/extensions;
- `runtime.mcp` server/tool state and reload;
- Runtime status/diagnostics and capability requirements;
- `tool.exposure.planned` events;
- External Agent dispatchable catalog and preflight;
- session/resume metadata when publicly available.

The capability source is surface-owned: Coder consumes the F121 daemon catalog/status projection, while Partner consumes the F116 embedded catalog plus explicit Space-owned MCP, Extension, policy, and External Agent bridges. The renderer never merges two owners into one apparently healthy capability.

Views:

- **Coder Task Dock summary**: healthy/degraded/blocked counts and the current Runtime isolation/effective policy.
- **Partner Process summary**: only current-run degradation, blocked requirements, retry/reconnect actions, and user-relevant recovery status.
- **Technical details**: capabilities available for this session/run, including policy-disabled reasons; MCP/Extension restored, missing, stale, or disabled state; resident/deferred/reachable Tool and Skill facts; External Agent dispatchability; and adapter/capability gaps.
- **Settings/Diagnostics**: project/app-wide health, catalog inspection, and supported reload/retry administration that is not specific to the current Partner run.

Actions are shown only when the Runtime advertises them. “Continue degraded” is descriptive and never silently broadens permissions.

### Acceptance

- Resume with a missing MCP/Extension produces a visible degraded reason.
- Reload/retry targets the Runtime owner and reports success/failure.
- Disabled-by-policy and unavailable-by-runtime are distinguishable.
- A2A/MCP Tasks/governed HTTP remain hidden or explicitly unsupported until conformant adapters exist.
- Diagnostics never reveal keys, endpoints with credentials, raw config secrets, or task content.
- Partner `Process` never exposes a permanent Skill/MCP inventory; its summary links to technical details when diagnosis is useful.

## FEATURE_091: Effort, Assurance, and Route Receipt

### User outcome

Users control effort and autonomy without understanding provider-specific reasoning flags, and can inspect the provider/model/effort/assurance facts that the Runtime truthfully exposes.

### Design

- Keep canonical Space effort choices mapped to KodaX effort.
- Keep autonomy (`plan`, `accept-edits`, `auto`) separate from effort.
- Compact receipt fields:
  - requested and effective effort;
  - provider/model;
  - permission/autonomy mode;
  - context window/budget state when public;
  - assurance class or verification requirement when public;
  - fallback/route facts when public.
- Unsupported combinations are disabled or explained from SDK capability metadata.
- In Partner, the compact Route receipt is reachable from `Process` technical details; provider/model names and fallback diagnostics do not compete with the result in the center or `Results`.
- A “why this route” sentence appears only when KodaX exports resolved route/fallback facts. Until then, Space says what is selected/effective, not why the engine chose it.
- Choosing, switching, or clearing an F130 outcome card may change only composer guidance, prompt playbook, and preferred result kind. It cannot change effort, autonomy, `permissionMode`, `autoModeEngine`, provider/model, file scope, connector grants, tool authorization, assurance, or review policy.
- KX-F265 can add assurance/cost fields later through additive capability negotiation.

### Explicit non-goals

- No task-class, `Plan`, or outcome selector that changes KodaX execution semantics.
- No Space-owned provider router or LLM classifier.
- No coupling between high effort and broad permissions.
- No fabricated explanation from model/provider names.

### Acceptance

- Effort and autonomy remain independent and persist with documented precedence.
- Effective provider/model/effort matches the Runtime session/run state.
- Fallback/degradation is visible when reported.
- Missing route/assurance contracts degrade to a smaller truthful receipt.
- Selecting every F130 outcome entry in turn leaves effective effort, autonomy, permission, model, and source scope unchanged.

## Release acceptance

- Coder Task Dock and Partner `Process` use one normalized fact projection while preserving their different information hierarchies.
- Partner `Results` remains the sole primary projection for generated objects and exports; task/capability receipts do not duplicate the full result in `Process`.
- Outcome, Completion, and Route receipts remain separately typed, owner-keyed, and rendered according to the cardinality above; persistence follows their owner, none implies another Space store, and none can be interpreted as a work-mode selection.
- Resume/degradation E2E covers missing MCP/Extension and provider fallback.
- No new durable store competes with Runtime-owned sessions, todos, catalog, or task ledger.
- Coder fixtures prove that Todo, catalog, status, route, and capability facts originate only from the F121 daemon projection; Partner fixtures prove that the same renderer model accepts only the F116 embedded-inline owner.
- No missing daemon capability can fall back to an inline Coder owner, and no Partner session can be admitted to the Coder daemon path.

## Rollout and rollback

- Gate plan history, capability actions, and route receipts separately; a missing resolved-route contract must not hide basic effort controls or the F130 Outcome receipt.
- Rollback removes Space projections/actions only. Runtime sessions, todos, catalog state, and task-ledger records remain authoritative and untouched.
- Unknown capability fields render as unavailable with diagnostics, never as false healthy/disabled states.
